# Setting Up Online Ordering for Your Shop: A Practical Walkthrough

Your customers are already ordering everything else online. If your shop doesn't offer it, you're not just missing sales — you're invisible to a whole category of buyer. Here's how to set up online ordering without drowning in complexity.

## Decide what "online ordering" means for you

It doesn't have to mean full e-commerce on day one. Common starting points:

- **Order ahead for pickup** — customer orders, you prepare it, they grab it. Great for food, coffee, and anything made-to-order.
- **Local delivery** — you deliver within your area. Works for groceries, gifts, pharmacy-type goods.
- **Ship it** — full shipping for customers anywhere. More setup, wider reach.
- **Request-a-quote** — for custom or high-value items, a simple "ask about this product" beats a full cart.

Start with the simplest version that serves your customers. You can always expand.

## Your product list is the foundation

Online ordering lives or dies on product information. Each item needs a clear name, an accurate price, and ideally a photo. You don't need professional photography — a clear phone photo in good light beats a stock image every time. Customers want to see what they're actually getting.

## Keep the menu/catalog current

Nothing kills trust faster than ordering something that's out of stock. If your online catalog is linked to your actual inventory, this problem mostly solves itself — items sell out online when they sell out in store. If it's manual, build a habit of updating it whenever stock changes significantly.

## Think through fulfillment

Before you launch, answer these: Who prepares online orders? Where in the shop? How do you know a new order came in — do you check constantly, or do you get a notification? What happens during a rush — do online orders jump the queue or join it? Walk through the whole flow physically once before going live.

## Pricing: keep it simple

Resist the urge to create a separate online price list. Same prices in-store and online keeps everything honest and avoids the "but your website says..." conversation. If delivery has a fee, state it plainly upfront — surprise fees at checkout are the top reason people abandon orders.

## Tell people it exists

The best online ordering setup in the world does nothing if nobody knows about it. Put it on your storefront window, your receipts, your social media, your Google listing. "Order ahead at [your site]" — say it everywhere for the first month until it sticks.
