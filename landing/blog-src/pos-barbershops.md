# POS for Barbershops: Booking, Retail, and the Chair-Side Till

A barbershop isn't just a retail counter — it's a service business with chairs to fill, appointments to manage, and product shelves to sell from. The right POS for a barbershop handles both sides: the booking book and the till, in one flow, without making the barber play receptionist.

Here's what to look for.

## What Barbershops Actually Need

Walk through a typical day and the requirements become clear:

- **Appointment booking** — clients book cuts, fades, beard trims, and kids' cuts, often outside business hours. Online booking that works while you sleep fills chairs you'd otherwise lose.
- **Chair-side checkout** — the transaction happens at the chair, not a front counter. The till needs to work on a phone or tablet, wherever the barber is standing.
- **Service menu with variants** — cut + beard combo, senior discount, first-time client pricing. The system should handle service variants as naturally as product SKUs.
- **Retail products** — pomade, clippers, beard oil. Product sales are high-margin revenue that many shops leave on the table for lack of easy tracking.
- **Staff tracking** — who cut whose hair, how many services per barber, commission calculations. Even in a booth-rental shop, service history matters.
- **Reminders** — no-shows kill a barber's day. Automatic appointment reminders (booked through the system) cut no-show rates dramatically.
- **Tips** — clients tip, and the system should handle tip recording cleanly at checkout.

## The Appointment Problem

Most generic POS systems treat appointments as an afterthought — a bolt-on module, a separate app, or a premium add-on. For a barbershop, appointments are the business. A booking system that's hard to use means:

- Clients default to phone calls, which interrupt cuts
- Double-bookings during busy periods
- No-shows with no reminder system
- Walk-in chaos on Saturdays

The booking flow should be: client picks a service, picks a barber, picks a time, gets a confirmation and a reminder. That's it. If it takes more steps than that, clients won't use it.

## Chair-Side Checkout in Practice

The traditional front-desk checkout creates a bottleneck: the client waits while the barber finishes up, walks to the counter, and pays. Chair-side checkout on a tablet or phone eliminates the wait and the awkwardness.

Requirements for it to work:

- **Mobile-friendly till** — full checkout on a phone screen, not a crippled "lite" version
- **Big touch targets** — you're operating it between cuts, possibly with product on your hands
- **Fast tender** — cash, card via your processor, and tip entry in under 30 seconds
- **Digital receipts** — no printer required at the chair

## Retail: The Overlooked Revenue

Most barbershops sell some retail — styling products, maintenance kits, gift sets — but track it informally or not at all. That's leaving money on the table twice: once in unmeasured shrinkage, once in missed reorders.

A proper inventory module changes this:

- **Track every unit** sold, with cost and margin visible
- **Low-stock alerts** before the best-selling pomade runs out on a Saturday
- **Bundle services with products** — "cut + take-home styling kit" as a single transaction
- **See what's actually profitable** — the sales report tells you whether retail is worth the shelf space

## Staff and Booth Rental Models

Barbershops organize labor in several ways — employees, commission splits, or booth rental — and the POS should accommodate all of them:

- **Service history per barber** — who performed what, for accurate commission or rental accounting
- **Time-clock** — for shops with hourly staff, shift tracking built into the same system
- **Individual logins** — each barber sees their own appointments and sales
