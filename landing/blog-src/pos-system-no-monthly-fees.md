# POS Systems with No Monthly Fees: How Flat Pricing Works

"How much does it cost per month?" is the wrong first question to ask about a POS system. The right question is: "How much does it cost per year, all in?" — because monthly pricing hides the fees that actually determine what you pay.

This guide explains POS systems with no monthly fees: how flat-fee pricing works, what to verify before committing, and whether the model fits your shop.

## The Monthly Fee Illusion

Monthly pricing feels manageable — $49/month sounds like less than $588/year, even though they're the same number. Providers know this, which is why the monthly figure is always the headline and the annual total is buried.

Worse, the monthly fee is rarely the whole story. The typical stack looks like this:

- Base monthly subscription
- Transaction percentage on every sale (the big one)
- Add-on modules (appointments, loyalty, advanced inventory)
- Per-register or per-location fees
- Hardware leases or purchases

A "$49/month" plan can easily cost $400+/month in practice once every layer is added. The monthly headline is marketing; the annual total is reality.

## "No Transaction Fees" — Read the Fine Print (Ours Is Short)

Any honest discussion of fees has to distinguish two things:

1. **Payment processing fees** — charged by Stripe, Lemon Squeezy, or whichever processor moves the money. These are standard industry rates, and every POS system in existence passes them through. Nobody can eliminate them.
2. **POS provider transaction fees** — an additional percentage some POS companies take on top of processing. This is pure margin for the software vendor, and it's entirely avoidable.

When Vendra says "no transaction fees," it means the second kind: Vendra takes $0 from your sales. You connect your own processor account, they charge their standard rate, and that's the only percentage anyone takes. Many providers take both — their cut plus the processor's — which is why the distinction matters.

## What to Verify in Any "No Monthly Fee" Claim

Flat pricing is only honest if it's actually flat. Before committing, confirm:

- **Is it really annual, or is there a monthly option that costs more?** True flat-fee systems bill annually, period.
- **Are all features included?** "No monthly fee" means little if core features are gated behind separate purchases.
- **How many registers and users?** Per-device fees are monthly fees wearing a disguise.
- **What happens at renewal?** Is the price locked, or can it jump? (Ask directly.)
- **What happens if you don't renew?** The honest answer: the system stops processing new sales, but nothing is deleted and you can always download your full backup.

Vendra's answers: annual billing only, every feature included, unlimited registers on your own devices, and lapsed accounts keep all data downloadable — the app simply locks new sales until renewal.

## Who Benefits Most from Flat Pricing

**High-volume, low-margin shops.** If you process a lot of transactions, percentage-based fees scale brutally. Flat pricing caps your software cost regardless of volume.

**Seasonal businesses.** A gift shop that does 60% of annual revenue in December shouldn't pay peak-season penalties to its software vendor. With flat pricing, December costs the same as February.

**Growing shops.** Adding a second register or location shouldn't trigger new fees. Flat pricing means expansion doesn't renegotiate your software bill.

**Budget-conscious owners.** One predictable annual expense is easier to plan around than a variable monthly bill that spikes with your best sales.
