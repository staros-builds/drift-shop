# POS for Coffee Shops: What to Look For (and What to Skip)

Coffee shops have specific needs that generic retail POS systems don't always handle well. High transaction volume, small ticket sizes, modifiers ("oat milk, extra shot, iced"), loyalty, and rush-hour speed — the wrong system slows down your baristas and irritates your regulars.

Here's what actually matters in a POS for coffee shops, and how to avoid paying for features you'll never use.

## Speed Is Everything

During the morning rush, every second at the till counts. A coffee shop POS needs:

- **One-tap favorite items.** Your top 10 drinks should be reachable in a single tap, not buried in menus.
- **Fast modifiers.** Milk alternatives, syrups, sizes, temperature — modifiers need to be quick to apply, not a five-screen ordeal.
- **Quick payment.** Contactless and mobile wallets should be the default path, not an afterthought.

If a demo of a POS system feels sluggish with ten items, imagine it with a line of twenty people. Test the till interface under pressure, not just in a calm demo.

## Modifiers and Customization

Coffee orders are rarely simple. "Large iced oat milk latte with an extra shot and sugar-free vanilla" is a normal order, not an edge case. Your POS should handle:

- **Modifier groups** (milk type, size, syrup, extras) that can be applied in any combination
- **Sensible defaults** so the common order is fast and the custom order is possible
- **Clear kitchen/bar display** so the barista sees exactly what was ordered, modifiers included

This is one area where a generic retail POS — designed for scanning barcodes — can fall short. Make sure the system you choose was designed with food and drink in mind, or at least handles modifiers gracefully.

## Loyalty and Repeat Customers

Coffee shops live on regulars. A good POS helps you keep them:

- **Simple loyalty tracking.** Buy-ten-get-one-free, points, or stored value — pick one and make it frictionless.
- **Customer profiles.** Remembering that someone always gets oat milk is a small touch that builds loyalty.
- **Gift cards.** Essential for the holidays and a nice revenue bump year-round.

## What Coffee Shops Usually Don't Need

POS vendors love to upsell. For a typical independent coffee shop, you can usually skip:

- **Complex multi-location management** (unless you're expanding)
- **Advanced purchase ordering and supplier management** (your needs are simpler than a grocery store's)
- **Built-in e-commerce** (nice to have, but your focus is foot traffic)

Don't pay enterprise prices for enterprise features you'll never touch.

## The Cost Question

Here's where it gets real. Many coffee-shop POS systems charge:

- $60–$150/month for the software
- 2.6–2.9% + per-transaction fees on every sale
- Extra for loyalty features, gift cards, or online ordering

For a shop doing $200,000 a year, that's easily $5,000–$7,000 in total annual costs. On coffee margins, that stings.

And remember: coffee shops have *high transaction counts with low average tickets*. Per-transaction flat fees (like 15¢ or 30¢ per sale) hit you harder than they hit a furniture store. A $4 latte with a 30¢ flat fee loses 7.5% before the percentage even kicks in.

## Inventory for Coffee Shops

Coffee shops have simpler inventory than most retail — but it still matters:

- **Track your high-cost items.** Specialty beans, syrups, and alternative milks add up. Knowing your actual usage vs. what you're ordering prevents waste.
- **Low-stock alerts** for the things that would shut you down if they ran out. Running out of oat milk during the morning rush is a crisis; running out of napkins is just annoying. Your POS should distinguish.
- **Waste tracking.** If you're throwing away pastries every evening, that's data. A POS with basic inventory helps you order smarter.
