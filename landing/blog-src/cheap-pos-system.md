# Cheap POS System: How to Get One Without Getting Burned

Searching for a cheap POS system is a little like shopping for a cheap car. The sticker price is only the beginning of the story. A $0/month point-of-sale plan can quietly cost you thousands a year once transaction fees, hardware markups, and "premium feature" upsells enter the picture.

This guide walks you through what actually makes a POS system cheap — and how to find one that stays cheap after the honeymoon period ends.

## Why "Free" POS Plans Are Rarely Cheap

Several major POS providers advertise a free tier. It sounds unbeatable until you run the numbers on what a small shop actually pays:

- **Transaction fees on every sale.** A "free" plan charging 2.6% + 15¢ per transaction costs a shop doing $150,000 a year roughly $4,000 in processing fees. The software is free; the payments are not.
- **Hardware lock-in.** Some providers only work with their own proprietary card readers and terminals, sold at a premium. Switching later means rebuying hardware.
- **Feature paywalls.** The free tier often excludes the things a real shop needs: inventory tracking, staff management, reporting. Those live in the $49–$149/month plans.

A cheap POS system isn't the one with the lowest advertised monthly price. It's the one with the lowest *total* cost of ownership: software + processing + hardware + add-ons, over a full year.

## What a Fair POS Price Looks Like

Before you evaluate any vendor, set a benchmark. Ask:

1. **What will I pay in software fees over 12 months?** Multiply the monthly price by 12. Watch for annual-only discounts that disappear on renewal.
2. **What will I pay in transaction fees?** Multiply your annual revenue by the provider's per-transaction rate. This is usually the biggest number.
3. **Do I need new hardware?** Some systems run on any tablet or phone you already own. Others require proprietary terminals.
4. **Are there per-register or per-location fees?** Multi-register shops can see costs double or triple.

When you add it up, many "affordable" POS systems cost $3,000–$7,000 a year for a modest shop. That's the number to beat.

## Features a Cheap POS Still Needs to Have

Going cheap shouldn't mean going without the basics. Any POS system you consider — budget or premium — should include:

- **A real till interface.** Big, touch-friendly buttons. Fast checkout. Discounts, refunds, and multiple payment types.
- **Inventory tracking.** Product catalog with stock levels and low-stock alerts. Without this, you're managing stock on spreadsheets.
- **Sales reporting.** Daily totals, top sellers, tax summaries. You need this for bookkeeping and for understanding your business.
- **Staff tools.** At minimum, individual logins and a time-clock so you know who's working when.
- **Data backup.** Your sales history and product catalog should be backed up automatically. Losing this data can be catastrophic.

Nice-to-haves that genuinely matter for small shops: appointment booking (for service businesses), online ordering, gift cards, and a simple public storefront page.

## The Hidden Costs to Watch For

When comparing cheap POS options, read the fine print for:

- **Payment processing markups.** Some providers require you to use *their* payment processing at above-market rates, with no option to bring your own processor.
- **Contract terms.** Multi-year contracts with early-termination fees turn a "cheap" system into an expensive trap.
- **Support tiers.** Phone support behind a paywall means you're on your own when the till goes down on a Saturday.
- **Data export fees or restrictions.** If leaving costs you your customer list, you were never really a customer — you were inventory.
