# Point of Sale System for Small Restaurants: A Buyer's Guide

Small restaurants — the independent bistro, the family taqueria, the neighborhood pizza place — need a point of sale system that handles food service without charging enterprise prices. The big restaurant POS platforms know this market well, and they price accordingly: steeply.

This guide covers what a small restaurant actually needs from a POS, what it can skip, and how to keep costs under control.

## What Small Restaurants Need

**Order management.** Dine-in, takeout, and (if applicable) delivery — tracked cleanly from order to payment. Table tracking for sit-down service.

**Menu modifiers.** "No onions, extra cheese, dressing on the side" — modifiers need to be fast and clear. This is non-negotiable in food service.

**Split checks and combined checks.** Parties split bills. Tables get rearranged. Your POS should handle this without a manager override every time.

**Staff management.** Time-clock, individual logins, and basic scheduling. In restaurants, labor is your biggest controllable cost — you need visibility.

**Online ordering.** Even if you don't do delivery, pickup orders placed online and fired straight to the kitchen save phone time and reduce errors.

**Gift cards.** Restaurants sell a huge share of gift cards during the holidays. Don't leave that money on the table.

## What Small Restaurants Can Usually Skip

- **Kitchen display systems with routing rules** — nice for large operations, overkill for a 10-table restaurant
- **Multi-location management** — until you actually have multiple locations
- **Advanced inventory with recipe costing** — useful eventually, but don't pay a premium for it on day one
- **Built-in payroll** — your accountant or a dedicated payroll service usually does this better

## The Pricing Problem

Restaurant POS pricing is some of the most aggressive in the industry:

- **Toast:** $69/month and up per terminal, plus payment processing at 2.49% + 15¢ (and you're required to use Toast's processing)
- **Square for Restaurants:** free to $60/month per location, plus 2.6% + 15¢ per transaction
- **Lightspeed Restaurant:** $89–$239/month, plus processing fees

Add required hardware (proprietary terminals), and a small restaurant can easily spend $5,000–$8,000 a year. For a business running on thin margins, that's painful.

Also watch for **long contracts**. Some restaurant POS vendors lock you into 2–3 year terms with expensive proprietary hardware leases. If the system doesn't work for you, leaving is costly.

## Key Questions for Restaurant POS Vendors

1. "Am I required to use your payment processing, or can I bring my own?"
2. "What is the contract term, and what does it cost to leave early?"
3. "Which features are included vs. add-ons?" (Online ordering and gift cards are commonly upsold.)
4. "Does it work on hardware I already own?"
5. "Can I export my menu, customer data, and sales history?"

## Reporting That Matters for Restaurants

You don't need a data science department, but you do need:

- **Sales by hour.** When are your peaks? This drives staffing decisions.
- **Top and bottom sellers.** Which menu items earn their place? Which should go?
- **Payment method breakdown.** How much are you paying in card fees? (This informs the processor question.)
- **Staff performance.** Who's upselling? Who needs coaching?

Clean, simple reports beat a 50-tab analytics dashboard you'll never open.

## Handling the Lunch and Dinner Rush

Restaurant rushes are the ultimate stress test for a POS. What to verify during your trial:

- **Can you fire multiple orders quickly?** During a rush, there's no time for a five-tap order entry.
- **Does the till stay responsive?** Some systems slow down with a large menu or many open orders. Test with your full menu loaded.
- **How are mistakes corrected?** Wrong item fired to the kitchen, wrong table charged — corrections should be fast and not require a manager for every fix.
- **Can staff split their attention?** Taking a new order while a previous table pays should be seamless, not a mode-switching nightmare.
