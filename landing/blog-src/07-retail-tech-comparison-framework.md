# Choosing Retail Tech: An Honest Framework (No Fake Comparisons)

Every retail tech vendor claims to be the best value. Most comparison articles are secretly sponsored. So here's something more useful than a ranked list: a framework for evaluating any point-of-sale or shop management system yourself.

## Start with your actual needs

Before looking at any product, write down what your shop actually does:

- Do you sell products, services, or both?
- Do you need appointments, or just walk-in sales?
- Do you have staff to track, or is it just you?
- Do you sell online, or plan to?
- How many languages do your customers speak?

A system that's perfect for a coffee shop might be wrong for a bike repair shop. Your needs list is the filter everything else passes through.

## The questions that actually matter

**What's the real total cost?** Not just the subscription — add up transaction fees, per-register fees, add-on modules, and hardware requirements. A "cheap" plan with a 2.9% transaction fee costs a busy shop thousands per year. A flat annual fee with no transaction cut is a completely different math. Do the arithmetic for *your* sales volume.

**What happens if you stop paying?** Some systems lock you out entirely — no access to your own sales history, customer list, or product catalog. Ask upfront: can you export your data? Can you download a backup? "Your data" should mean yours.

**Does it work the way you work?** A 20-minute demo tells you more than 20 review sites. Run a real sale. Process a refund. Look up a product. If it feels awkward in the demo, it'll feel worse at 6pm on a Saturday with a line.

**Who owns the company?** This sounds odd, but it matters: venture-backed companies get acquired, sunset products, and jack up prices. Smaller independent vendors live or die on keeping you happy. Neither is automatically better — just know which you're buying into.

**Can you leave?** Check the contract for lock-in periods, data export options, and hardware compatibility. The best time to think about leaving a platform is before you join it.

## Red flags

- Pricing you can't find without talking to sales
- "Free" plans that charge per transaction at high rates
- No data export or backup option
- Reviews that all sound the same (possibly incentivized)
- Feature lists that are mostly "coming soon"

## Green flags

- Transparent, published pricing
- Free trial that doesn't ask for a credit card trap
- Data export / backup you control
- Real human support
- A changelog that shows the product is actively maintained
