# Affordable POS Systems for Small Business: What to Look For (and What to Avoid)

Running a small shop means watching every dollar. So when a point-of-sale provider quotes you a monthly fee, a per-transaction percentage, and add-ons for basic features, it pays to step back and ask: what am I actually getting for this money?

This guide breaks down how POS pricing really works, which features a small business genuinely needs, and how to find an affordable POS system without getting locked into fees that grow with your revenue.

## Why POS Software Is So Expensive

Most major POS providers price their products for mid-size and enterprise clients, not independents. The pricing model usually has three layers:

1. **Monthly subscription fee** — typically tiered, with the cheapest plans lacking core features.
2. **Transaction percentage** — a cut of every sale, on top of your payment processor's own fee.
3. **Add-ons and hardware** — gift cards, loyalty, appointments, and staff management often cost extra.

For a shop doing $150,000 a year in revenue, this stack can easily exceed $5,000 annually. That's a second rent payment — just to ring up sales.

## What a Small Business Actually Needs

Before comparing plans, list your real requirements. Most independent shops need:

- **A simple, touch-friendly till** that staff can learn in under an hour
- **Inventory tracking** with low-stock alerts so you never run out mid-day
- **A customer directory** to remember repeat buyers
- **Basic reporting** — daily totals, best sellers, slow movers
- **Appointment booking** if you offer services
- **Staff time-clock** if you have a team
- **Online ordering** so customers can buy when the shop is closed
- **Automatic backups** because losing your sales history would be a disaster

Notice what isn't on that list: enterprise analytics dashboards, multi-warehouse logistics, franchise management. You shouldn't pay for software built for someone else's problems.

## The Transaction Fee Trap

The most expensive part of a POS contract is often the least visible: the transaction percentage. A provider taking 2.6% of every sale sounds small until you do the math — on $200,000 in annual revenue, that's $5,200 a year, every year, forever. It scales with your success: the more you sell, the more you pay.

Your payment processor (Stripe, Lemon Squeezy, etc.) charges its own standard processing fee regardless of which POS you use. The question is whether your POS provider *also* takes a cut on top. An affordable POS system charges you for the software and takes nothing from your sales.

## Features That Save Real Money

When evaluating an affordable POS system, look for included features that would otherwise require separate subscriptions:

- **Online ordering built in** — eliminates the need for a separate ordering platform with its own commission
- **A public storefront page** — replaces a basic website builder subscription for many shops
- **Appointment booking** — replaces standalone scheduling tools
- **Gift cards** — a revenue driver that some providers gate behind premium tiers

Five separate subscriptions at $15-30/month each add up to $900-$1,800/year. One platform that includes them all changes the economics completely.

## Questions to Ask Before You Sign Up

1. **What is my total annual cost, including transaction fees?** Make them do the math on your revenue, not theirs.
2. **What happens if I don't renew?** Can you still access your data? Can you download your full backup? Data should never be held hostage.
3. **Is there a free trial with full access?** You should test every feature with your real workflow before paying.
4. **Does it work on hardware I already own?** Proprietary hardware lock-in is a hidden cost — tablets and phones you already have should be enough.
5. **What languages does it support?** If your staff or customers aren't English-first, multilingual support matters daily.
6. **Who do I contact when something breaks?** Small businesses can't afford a three-day support ticket queue.
