# Setting Up Your Shop's Online Storefront: A Step-by-Step Guide

A storefront page is your shop's front door on the internet. It's what shows up when someone searches for what you sell, and it's often the first impression a new customer gets. Here's how to set one up properly.

## Step 1: Claim your name

Your storefront URL should be findable and memorable. If you can get yourshopname on a platform, take it. Consistency matters — use the same name everywhere (Google, social media, directories) so customers always find the real you.

## Step 2: Write a description that says what you do

Not "welcome to our website." Say what you sell, who it's for, and where you are. "Independent bookshop in Ottawa — new and used books, special orders, open late Thursdays." One or two sentences. If a stranger can't tell what your shop does in five seconds, rewrite it.

## Step 3: List your real products

A storefront with no products is a signpost pointing nowhere. Start with your best sellers — ten products is plenty to begin with. Each needs: a name, a price, and a photo. Add more over time. A small, current catalog beats a huge, outdated one.

## Step 4: Put your hours and location front and center

This is the most-looked-at information on any local shop page and the most commonly buried. Hours, address, phone — visible without scrolling, on every page. If your hours change for holidays, update them *before* the holiday, not after the angry reviews.

## Step 5: Make contacting you effortless

Phone number, email, contact form, messaging — offer at least two. And actually monitor them. A contact form nobody checks is worse than no contact form, because it creates an expectation you then disappoint.

## Step 6: Show you're alive

A storefront that hasn't changed in two years looks abandoned, even if the physical shop is thriving. You don't need a blog — just update something periodically: new arrivals, seasonal hours, a photo of the window display. Signs of life build confidence.

## Step 7: Connect it to everything else

Your storefront should link to your social profiles, your Google listing, your online ordering if you have it. And all of those should link back. Every connection is another path for a customer to find you and another signal to search engines that you're real.

## The technical bit (simpler than you think)

You don't need to hire a web developer. Modern shop platforms include a public storefront as part of the package — your products, your branding, your URL structure, without touching code. If your current system doesn't offer one, that's worth weighing the next time you evaluate your tools.
