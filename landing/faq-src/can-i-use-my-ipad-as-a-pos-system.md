# Can I Use My iPad as a POS System?

**Yes — your iPad works great as a POS system.** Most modern POS software, including Vendra, Square, and Lightspeed, runs in a web browser or as an app on iPad. You just need the POS software, a card reader that connects via Bluetooth or Lightning/USB-C, and optionally a receipt printer and cash drawer.

## What You Need to Turn an iPad Into a POS

**The essentials:**
- **iPad** (any model from the last 5–6 years works fine — you don't need the latest Pro)
- **POS software** — browser-based systems like Vendra work on any iPad with Safari; no app install needed
- **Card reader** — Bluetooth readers ($49–$99) pair with the iPad wirelessly; plug-in readers connect via the charging port

**Nice to have:**
- **iPad stand** — countertop stands run $30–$150 and make the setup look professional
- **Receipt printer** — Bluetooth thermal printers ($100–$200)
- **Cash drawer** — connects to the receipt printer, opens automatically on cash sales ($80–$120)

Total hardware cost for a solid iPad POS setup: roughly $200–$500, versus $800–$1,500+ for proprietary POS terminals.

## Browser-Based vs. App-Based POS

**Browser-based (like Vendra):** Opens in Safari, no download needed, works on any iPad (or Android tablet, laptop, or phone). Updates happen automatically. If the iPad breaks, log in from any other device and keep selling.

**App-based (like Square):** Requires downloading the app from the App Store. Tied to Apple's ecosystem and update cycle. Generally works well but locks you into iPad specifically.

Browser-based is more flexible — the same system works on whatever device you have handy.

## What About Older iPads?

If your iPad can run a current version of Safari or Chrome, it can run a browser-based POS. That covers iPads going back to roughly 2017–2018. Before buying new hardware, try the POS software's free trial on the iPad you already own.

## iPad POS Limitations to Know

- **One screen** means the cashier and customer share the display (customer-facing displays cost extra)
- **Battery** — keep the iPad plugged in during business hours
- **Wi-Fi dependency** — most iPad POS systems need internet; check whether your system handles offline sales

## Can One iPad Handle a Busy Shop?

For most independent retailers — boutiques, bookstores, cafés, salons — a single iPad is plenty. The checkout process is fast: tap products, take payment, print or email the receipt. Where shops run into trouble is trying to do inventory management and sales on the same screen during a rush. The practical fix is simple: use the iPad for checkout during busy hours and handle back-office tasks (reordering, reports) on a laptop before or after the rush.

If you regularly have lines at the till, a second iPad as an additional register costs far less than a proprietary second terminal — and both run the same Vendra account with synced inventory.
