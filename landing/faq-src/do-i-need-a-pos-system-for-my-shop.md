# Do I Need a POS System for My Shop?

**Yes — if your shop takes card payments, tracks inventory, or has more than a handful of products, you need a POS system.** A modern POS does far more than process payments: it tracks stock levels, records every sale, generates reports, and saves hours of manual bookkeeping each week.

## What a POS System Actually Does

Many shop owners think "POS" just means "card reader." In reality, a point-of-sale system is your shop's central nervous system:

- **Processes sales** — card, cash, gift card, and split payments with proper receipts
- **Tracks inventory** — stock counts update automatically with every sale, with alerts before you run out
- **Records everything** — every transaction is logged, so tax time and bookkeeping become straightforward
- **Generates reports** — see your best sellers, slowest hours, and busiest days at a glance
- **Manages staff** — time-clock, permissions, and individual sales tracking

## Signs You've Outgrown the Cash Box

You probably need a POS if any of these sound familiar:

- You're counting stock by hand and still running out of popular items
- Tax season means reconstructing a year of sales from bank statements
- You can't tell which products actually make money
- Staff handle cash with no individual accountability
- Customers ask to pay by card and you can't accept it (or use a separate reader with no integration)

## What About Very Small Shops?

Even a weekend market stall benefits from a basic POS. The question isn't whether you need one — it's how much you should pay. A micro-business doesn't need a $6,000/year enterprise system. But going without means no sales records, no inventory tracking, and no data to grow on.

## What Happens If You Keep Going Without One?

Shops that delay getting a POS usually hit the same wall: growth makes manual methods collapse. The weekend you do record sales, you can't reconcile the cash drawer. The month you add a second employee, you have no way to track who sold what. Tax season becomes a forensic exercise instead of a report export.

The cost of not having a POS isn't zero — it's the hours you spend on manual work, the stockouts you could have prevented, the tax deductions you can't document, and the sales you lose when a customer walks away because you can't take their card.
