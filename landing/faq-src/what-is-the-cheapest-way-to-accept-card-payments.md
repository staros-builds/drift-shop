## Understanding the Two Layers of Card Fees

Every card payment has two potential layers of fees, and most shop owners only think about one:

**Layer 1: The processor's fee.** This goes to the company that actually moves the money (Stripe, Square Payments, etc.). Typical rates are 2.6–2.9% + $0.15–$0.30 per transaction. This fee is unavoidable — someone has to process the payment.

**Layer 2: The POS platform's cut.** Many POS providers take an additional percentage or per-transaction fee on top of the processor's rate. Some bake it into higher processing rates; others charge it as a separate line item. This is pure margin for the software company.

## Where the Money Actually Goes

On a $40 sale:
- Processor takes ~$1.16–$1.46 (their standard rate)
- Some POS providers take an additional cut on top
- You keep the rest

Over $200,000 in annual sales, even a 0.5% POS markup costs you $1,000/year. A 2.6% all-in rate (common with bundled POS + processing) costs $5,200/year in transaction fees alone — before any monthly software subscription.

## What About "Free" Card Readers?

Free or cheap card readers usually come with higher transaction rates or monthly minimums. Do the math on your actual volume — a $49 reader with lower rates beats a free reader with a 3.5% rate if you process more than a few thousand dollars a month.

## Negotiating Better Processing Rates

If you're already processing significant volume ($10k+/month), you have leverage:

- **Ask your processor for interchange-plus pricing** instead of flat-rate — it's more transparent and often cheaper at volume
- **Get competing quotes** — processors will sharpen their pencils when they know you're shopping
- **Watch for the real rate, not the headline** — "2.6%" might exclude the per-transaction fixed fee, monthly minimums, or PCI compliance charges
- **Separate the negotiation** — negotiate processing rates independently from POS software; bundled deals hide the true cost of each

Even with negotiated rates, though, the processor's fee is only half the equation. If your POS adds its own percentage on top, you're still overpaying. The shops with the lowest total card-acceptance costs use a competitive processor paired with $0-fee POS software.

## Mobile and Online Payments

The same two-layer logic applies to online sales and mobile wallets (Apple Pay, Google Pay). Your processor charges its rate for these transactions; your POS should not add a surcharge for the payment method. Some providers charge higher rates for online vs. in-person transactions — another line item to check when comparing.

The cheapest setup is consistent across channels: one competitive processor, one $0-fee POS platform, no per-method markups.
