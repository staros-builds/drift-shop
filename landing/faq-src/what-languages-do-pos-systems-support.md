## The Language Gap in Retail Software

Walk into any small shop in Montreal, Miami, Los Angeles, or São Paulo and you'll hear multiple languages. The staff might speak Spanish, the owner might prefer English, and customers might need French. But most POS software was designed in California for English-speaking users, and other languages were an afterthought — if they were considered at all.

**Typical language support among major POS providers:**
- **Square:** English primarily; limited Spanish and French in some markets
- **Lightspeed:** English and French (stronger in Canada), limited elsewhere
- **Shopify POS:** English primarily; some additional languages depending on region
- **Clover/Toast:** English primarily

In many cases, "support" means a partially translated interface — the checkout screen might be in Spanish but the reports, settings, and help documentation stay in English.

## Why This Matters for Real Shops

Language isn't a nice-to-have in retail — it's operational:

- **Staff training** is faster and safer when the till speaks the cashier's language (fewer mis-keyed sales)
- **Customer trust** improves when receipts and the checkout display match the customer's language
- **Quebec's language expectations** make French support effectively mandatory for customer-facing businesses
- **Hiring** opens up when language isn't a barrier to using the till — you hire the best person, not the best English speaker

## What Full Multilingual Support Looks Like

True multilingual support means:

1. **The entire interface** — not just the sales screen, but inventory, reports, settings, and staff management
2. **Per-user settings** — the owner sees English, the cashier sees Spanish, and neither has to compromise
3. **No extra charge** — language shouldn't be a premium tier or paid add-on
4. **Help documentation** in each language, not just machine-translated marketing pages

## Testing Language Support Before You Commit

Don't take the marketing page at face value. During your trial:

1. **Switch the interface** to each language your team needs and click through every major screen — sales, inventory, reports, settings
2. **Print a test receipt** in each language and check it's complete and correct
3. **Have a native speaker try the checkout flow** — awkward translations cause real errors at the till
4. **Check the help docs** — if support articles are English-only, your non-English-speaking staff are on their own when something goes wrong
5. **Test per-user settings** — can two users really use different languages simultaneously, or does switching affect everyone?

A 30-day trial is enough time to run this full check. If the vendor's multilingual support doesn't survive a real-world test with your actual team, it's not real support.

## The Regulatory Angle

In some jurisdictions, language support isn't optional. Quebec's Charter of the French Language sets expectations for French in commercial communications. While POS software requirements specifically can be nuanced, customer-facing businesses in Quebec operate most safely with full French support — receipts, displays, and interfaces your customers interact with.

Beyond compliance, there's a simpler argument: your staff and customers deserve software that speaks their language. It's a basic respect issue, and it's table stakes for any POS claiming to serve independent retailers in multilingual markets.
