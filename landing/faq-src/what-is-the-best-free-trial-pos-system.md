## Free Trial vs. Free Plan: Know the Difference

These sound similar but work very differently:

**Free trial** = full product, time-limited. You get every feature for 14–30 days, then decide whether to pay. This is the honest way to evaluate software.

**Free plan** = limited product, unlimited time. You get a stripped-down version forever. The limitations (fewer features, higher transaction fees, no support) are designed to push you toward a paid tier.

A free plan can be fine for a hobby business, but don't mistake it for a trial — you're not seeing what the paid product actually does.

## What to Look for in a POS Trial

**1. Full feature access.** If the trial hides inventory management, reporting, or staff features behind "upgrade to try," you can't properly evaluate it. The trial should be the real product.

**2. No credit card upfront.** Requiring a card before you've decided is a dark pattern — it counts on you forgetting to cancel. A confident company doesn't need your card until you're ready to pay.

**3. Enough time.** 7 days isn't enough to evaluate a POS. You need at least 2–4 weeks to set up products, train staff, process real sales, and see reports. 30 days is the sweet spot.

**4. Real sales, not sandbox only.** The trial should let you process actual transactions (even if you start with a few test sales). A demo environment with fake data doesn't tell you how the system handles your rush hour.

**5. Data portability.** If you decide not to continue, can you export what you entered? A trial that holds your product catalog hostage isn't a trial — it's a trap.
