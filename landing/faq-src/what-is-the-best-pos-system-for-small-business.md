## What Actually Makes a POS "Best" for Small Business

The "best" POS depends on your shop, but the criteria are the same for almost every independent retailer:

**1. True annual cost.** Don't look at the monthly fee alone. Add up 12 months of subscription fees plus transaction fees on your annual revenue. A "$0/month" plan charging 2.6% per sale costs a $200k-revenue shop over $5,000/year. Always do the full math before committing.

**2. Features you actually use.** Most small shops need: a fast checkout screen, inventory tracking with low-stock alerts, sales reports, and the ability to accept card payments. Appointment booking and staff time-tracking matter if you run a salon, barbershop, or service business. Don't pay for enterprise features you'll never touch.

**3. Ease of use.** If your staff can't learn it in under an hour, it's the wrong system. Look for big touch-friendly buttons, plain-language labels, and a setup process that doesn't require a technician visit.

**4. Language support.** If your team or customers speak French, Spanish, or Portuguese, make sure the POS handles it natively — not as a paid add-on.

**5. No lock-in.** Avoid long contracts and proprietary hardware that traps you. You should be able to run the POS on hardware you already own and leave whenever you want.

## Red Flags When Evaluating POS Systems

Watch out for these warning signs during your search:

- **"Free" plans with the highest transaction rates** — the free tier exists to get you in the door; the rates ensure you pay more than a subscriber would
- **Required proprietary hardware** — if you can only use their card readers and terminals, you're locked in
- **Contract terms over 12 months** — legitimate SaaS doesn't need multi-year lock-in; it needs a good product
- **No published pricing** — "contact sales" usually means "expensive enough that we'd rather talk you into it"
- **Per-register or per-location fees** — these multiply your bill silently as you grow
