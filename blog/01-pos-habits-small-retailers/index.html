Your point of sale is the heartbeat of your shop. Every sale, every refund, every end-of-day count runs through it. But most small retailers only use a fraction of what their POS can do. Here are five habits that make the till work for you instead of the other way around.

1. Ring up by product, not by price

It's tempting to punch in "$12.99" and move on, especially during a rush. But when every sale is tied to an actual product in your inventory, your stock counts stay honest automatically. A sale isn't just money in — it's one unit out. Build the habit of scanning or selecting the product every time, even when the line is long. Future you, doing stocktake at midnight, will be grateful.

2. Close the till the same way every day

Pick a routine and stick to it: count the drawer, record the total, note any discrepancies, done. A consistent close-out takes five minutes and catches problems early — a miscounted float, a voided sale someone forgot to log, a refund that never got recorded. Shops that skip this end up doing forensic accounting at month-end instead.

3. Learn your void and refund flow cold

Every POS handles voids and refunds slightly differently, and fumbling through it with a customer waiting is stressful for everyone. Practice on a quiet afternoon: void a sale, process a refund, reprint a receipt. When it happens for real — and it will — you'll handle it in seconds.

4. Use the customer display if you have one

Even a basic customer-facing screen that shows what's being rung up builds trust. Customers catch mistakes ("wait, I didn't get the large") before they pay, which means fewer refunds and fewer awkward corrections. If your setup supports it, turn it on.

5. Back up your sales data

Your sales history is business intelligence: what sells, when, to whom. Make sure it's backed up somewhere you control. If your POS offers a data export or backup download, use it regularly — weekly at minimum. Hardware fails, accounts get locked, companies change pricing. Your data shouldn't be hostage to any of that.
