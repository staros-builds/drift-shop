Every retail year has its peaks — holidays, back-to-school, tourist season, local festivals. The shops that thrive during rushes are the ones that prepared during the quiet weeks. Here's a practical prep checklist.

6-8 weeks out: plan inventory

Look at last year's sales for the same season. What sold out? What gathered dust? Order the winners early — suppliers get slammed too, and "out of stock" from your distributor in peak week is a disaster. For new products you're testing, order conservatively; you can reorder, but you can't un-order.

4-6 weeks out: staff up and train

If you need seasonal help, hire before you desperately need them. Two weeks of training during normal pace beats two days of panic-training during the rush. Even for existing staff, do a refresher: the POS void/refund flow, the gift-wrap station, the "where do we put the extra stock" plan.

3-4 weeks out: set up the shop

Decorations, signage, seasonal displays — do it once, do it properly. Plan your layout for crowds: wider aisles, the register area clear, impulse buys near the till. Walk the shop as a customer and notice where bottlenecks will form.

2 weeks out: systems check

Test everything: POS, card reader, receipt printer, online ordering, your website hours. Replace the receipt paper *now*. Charge the backup card reader. There's never a good time for the till to die, but peak season is the worst time.

1 week out: communicate

Update your hours everywhere — website, Google, social media, the door sign. Post about extended hours, special seasonal products, ordering deadlines for delivery. Customers plan around this information; stale info creates real frustration.

During the rush: protect the basics

When it's hectic, the fundamentals matter most: keep the shop tidy (a messy shop during a rush looks chaotic, not busy), keep staff fed and rested (tired staff make mistakes and snap at customers), and keep the till area moving (that's where the money happens — don't let it become the bottleneck).

After: review while it's fresh

The week after the rush, write down what worked and what didn't. What sold out too fast? Where did the line back up? What broke? Next year's prep starts with this year's notes. Memory fades; the document doesn't.
