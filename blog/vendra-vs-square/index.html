If you're comparing POS systems, Square is probably on your list. It's the best-known name in small-business payments, and for good reason — it made card acceptance simple when that was genuinely hard.

But "best-known" and "best for your shop" aren't the same thing. Here's an honest comparison of Vendra vs Square, including where Square wins.

Pricing: The Biggest Difference

Square uses a freemium model: the basic POS software is free, but you pay per transaction (around 2.6% + a flat per-sale fee on in-person payments). Paid plans ($29–$60+/month per location in their current tiers) lower the transaction rate slightly and add features.

Vendra charges $350/year flat — no monthly fee, and no transaction fee from Vendra. You connect your own payment processor (like Stripe), which charges its standard processing rates.

What this means in practice: on Square's free plan, a shop's software cost scales directly with revenue — the more you sell, the more you pay, forever. With Vendra, your software cost is fixed regardless of how much you sell.

For a shop doing $200,000/year, Square's transaction fees alone typically run into the thousands annually. Vendra's total software cost stays at $350. That's the core trade-off, and it's worth calculating for your own numbers.

Features

Where Square has an edge:

  • Ecosystem maturity. Square has been around longer, with a larger app marketplace and more third-party integrations.
  • Hardware options. Square sells its own well-designed readers, stands, and terminals.
  • Brand recognition. Customers recognize the Square name, which can build trust.

Where Vendra holds its own:

  • Everything included. POS till, inventory management, appointment booking, staff time-clock and scheduling, online ordering, public storefront page, gift cards, and reporting — all in the $350/year price. Square spreads comparable capabilities across paid tiers and add-ons.
  • Languages. Vendra ships in English, French, Spanish, and Brazilian Portuguese. Square's language support is more limited.
  • No payment lock-in. Square requires you to use Square's payment processing. Vendra lets you connect your own processor.

Where they're similar:

  • Both run on tablets and phones you already own.
  • Both offer a free trial period to test the software.
  • Both include basic sales reporting.

Ease of Use

Both systems aim for simplicity. Vendra was designed specifically to be beginner-proof — big touch buttons, plain-language labels, no unexplained jargon. The design bar: if you can use a smartphone, you can run your shop on Vendra.

Square's interface is also well-regarded for usability. This one's close; try both and see which feels more natural to you and your staff.

Who Should Choose Square

Square may be the better fit if:

  • You want the largest possible ecosystem of integrations and add-ons
  • You prefer Square's proprietary hardware
  • Your transaction volume is very low (where per-transaction fees stay small in absolute terms)
  • You value brand recognition with customers

Who Should Choose Vendra

Vendra may be the better fit if:

  • You want predictable, flat pricing that doesn't grow with your revenue
  • You don't want to pay a percentage of every sale to your software vendor
  • You need multiple languages (especially French, Spanish, or Portuguese)
  • You want everything included in one price — no tier-hopping for basic features
  • You prefer to choose your own payment processor

Switching Costs: What to Expect

Moving from Square to anything else (or vice versa) involves real work:

  • Hardware. Square's proprietary readers and stands won't work with other systems. Budget for new hardware or choose a system that runs on generic tablets.
  • Data migration. Export your products, customers, and sales history from Square before cancelling. Import them into the new system and verify.
  • Staff retraining. Even if the new system is simpler, there's a learning curve. Plan for a few days of adjustment.
  • Payment processing. If you switch away from Square, you'll set up a new processor (like Stripe). There's usually no downtime if you plan it, but don't do it on a Friday afternoon.

None of these are deal-breakers, but they're worth planning for. The savings usually justify the effort within the first year.

The Honest Bottom Line

Square is a legitimate, well-built product from an established company. It's not a scam and it's not junk. For very small or brand-new businesses with minimal sales, Square's free tier can genuinely be the cheapest option — paying 2.6% on $20,000 of annual sales is about $520, which is competitive.

The math shifts as you grow. Once you're doing meaningful revenue, a flat $350/year with no transaction cut starts winning by a wide margin — and the gap grows every year.
