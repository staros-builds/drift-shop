Canada's language laws aren't suggestions — in Quebec, French-language requirements for business are real, and across the country, shops serve customers and employ staff in both official languages. A POS system that only speaks English is a liability, not a tool.

Here's what Canadian shops need from a bilingual POS, and how to evaluate your options.

Why Bilingual Matters in Canadian Retail

In Quebec: The Charter of the French Language requires that commercial signage, product labelling, and customer-facing communications be in French. While POS software itself sits in a grey area, a system that can't display French to customers and staff creates compliance risk and practical headaches.

Everywhere else: Canada's workforce is multilingual. A shop in Toronto might have staff who prefer English, Spanish, or Portuguese. A store in Vancouver serves customers in English and French (and often Mandarin or Punjabi). A POS that locks everyone into one language doesn't reflect how Canadian shops actually operate.

The practical reality: When your cashier, your manager, and your customer all prefer different languages, the POS should adapt — not force everyone into English.

What to Look for in a Bilingual POS

1. Full interface translation, not just receipts. Some systems print bilingual receipts but keep the actual till interface English-only. Your staff needs the *whole system* in their language — buttons, menus, settings, error messages.

2. Per-user language settings. The owner might prefer English while the cashier prefers French. The system should let each user choose, without affecting anyone else.

3. French tax handling. Quebec's QST/GST (TVQ/TPS) structure needs proper support. Make sure the POS handles Quebec sales tax correctly — this is non-negotiable.

4. Bilingual receipts. Customers in Quebec expect French (or bilingual) receipts. The POS should generate them automatically.

5. Support in French. When something breaks on a Saturday, you need help in your language — not a chatbot that only understands English.

The Problem With Most POS Systems

The major POS platforms were built in the US for the US market. French support, where it exists, is often:

  • A partial translation with English mixed in
  • Limited to the customer-facing display, not the back office
  • An enterprise-tier feature, not available on standard plans
  • Missing Quebec-specific tax handling

Canadian shops end up paying full price for software that treats their language as an afterthought.

Beyond Quebec: Bilingual Needs Across Canada

The bilingual POS conversation often centres on Quebec, but the need is national:

  • Ontario: French-language services are legally required in designated areas. Shops near Ottawa serve a genuinely bilingual customer base.
  • New Brunswick: Canada's only officially bilingual province. A POS that can't do both languages is a non-starter for many businesses here.
  • Western Canada: Shops in Vancouver, Calgary, and Edmonton serve diverse communities. Spanish and Portuguese support matters for staff as much as French does for customers.

A POS with four built-in languages covers far more of Canada's real retail landscape than an English-only system with a French "language pack" sold separately.
