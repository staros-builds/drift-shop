Square introduced millions of small businesses to modern point-of-sale software. But as independent retailers grow — or as their margins get squeezed — many start asking whether there's a better fit: something simpler, cheaper, and built for shops rather than scaled for enterprises.

This guide is for independent retailers evaluating Square alternatives in 2026: what to look for, what to watch out for, and how to compare options honestly.

Why Retailers Look Beyond Square

Square is a fine product for many sellers, especially those just starting out. But independent retailers commonly cite the same frustrations:

  • Monthly costs plus a percentage of every sale — the bill grows automatically with revenue, which punishes your best months.
  • Feature sprawl — the ecosystem keeps expanding toward larger businesses, and the simple experience gets buried under upsells.
  • Add-on pricing — appointments, loyalty, marketing, and advanced inventory features often live in higher tiers or separate products.
  • One-size-fits-all — the product is designed for everyone from food trucks to franchises, which means it's optimized for no one in particular.

None of these are fatal flaws. They're just signs that the product's center of gravity has moved away from the independent shop.

What "Independent Retailer" Actually Means Here

For this guide, an independent retailer is a locally owned shop — a bookstore, boutique, gift shop, record store, pet supply shop, or similar — typically with 1-10 employees and under $1M in annual revenue. These shops share specific needs:

  • Staff who need to learn the system in minutes, not days
  • Inventory in the hundreds or low thousands of SKUs, not hundreds of thousands
  • Personal customer relationships worth tracking simply
  • Tight margins where every fee matters
  • Owners who do their own books and don't have an IT department

How to Compare Square Alternatives

Don't compare feature checklists — compare total cost and daily workflow.

Total annual cost. Add the subscription for 12 months plus the transaction percentage applied to your actual revenue. This is the number that matters, and it's the number most comparison pages avoid calculating for you.

Transaction fees. Distinguish between your payment processor's fee (unavoidable — Stripe and others charge standard rates) and the POS provider's additional cut. Some alternatives charge $0 on top of processing.

Included vs. gated features. Check whether appointments, gift cards, staff management, online ordering, and multi-language support are included or upsold.

Hardware requirements. Can you run it on tablets and phones you already own, or does it require proprietary terminals?

Data ownership. If you cancel, can you export everything? Is there a backup download? Your sales history is a business asset — never accept a platform that holds it hostage.

Language support. In multilingual markets, staff-facing language options aren't a nice-to-have. If your team works in Spanish or French, the till should too.

4. Train staff during quiet hours — a beginner-friendly interface should take under an hour.
